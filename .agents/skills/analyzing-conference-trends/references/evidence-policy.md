# Evidence and comparison policy

Read this reference before writing trends, cross-venue comparisons, advances, or paper-result claims.

## Claim types

Every substantive claim uses one evidence type and source URLs:

| Type | Allowed content | Locator rule |
|---|---|---|
| `official_metadata` | Acceptance, track, authors, award status, program facts | Identify the official page or artifact. |
| `paper_reported` | Methods, data, evaluation, limitations, results | Numerical claims require a paper section, table, figure, or page locator and the original setting. |
| `cross_paper_synthesis` | A conclusion supported across named papers | Link the supporting paper set; state the synthesis boundary. |
| `inference` | A reasoned implication not directly reported | Label it explicitly and link the observations it uses. |

Do not turn missing metadata into a negative finding. Report coverage and nulls.

## Distribution versus trend

A one-year result is a distribution, snapshot, or hotspot, never evidence of growth by itself. Trend claims normally require at least three consecutive years under comparable scope and taxonomy. A shorter window must state its years and be described as a limited comparison, not a stable trend.

## Cross-venue comparison

Raw paper counts are not evidence of venue interest or trend because conference sizes and track boundaries differ. Retain counts for transparency, then compare a shared taxonomy using:

- topic share: topic papers / included venue-year papers;
- year-over-year share delta in percentage points;
- venue enrichment: venue topic share / pooled topic share; or
- cross-venue spread: count and share of configured venues containing the topic.

Publish denominators, scope, taxonomy version, formula components, and missingness. Never rank by an Emerging Score without publishing its formula and component values.

## Advance synthesis

Organize supported advances under Text LLMs, Multimodal Models, Reasoning and Agents, Data/Pretraining/Post-training, and Evaluation/Safety/Interpretability. Cite representative papers, distinguish synthesis from inference, and avoid equating acceptance volume with technical progress.
